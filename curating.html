Curating About

Curatorial practice

Working with space, research and memory.

My approach to curating is rooted in spatial thinking, documentary methods, and alternative archiving. I believe curatorial and artistic practice are interconnected forms of inquiry. Both begin with conversations, site-specific investigation, and sustained research.

Research as a starting point

I believe strong artistic and curatorial projects should start with urgent questions rather than broad themes. Each project begins with a specific research question. To pursue that inquiry, I begin outside the studio and gallery through in-depth interviews, archival and field research, extended conversations with artists, participants and communities, and site visits. Only after this rigorous foundation is built do I translate these findings into exhibitions and public encounters.

I engage with artworks, archives, collections, and institutions not as static entities but as sites of ongoing reconstruction.

Exhibition as spatial writing

My background in architectural engineering profoundly influences my approach to exhibitions. I don’t see them as empty spaces but as living structures that shape the relationship between artworks and the people who move through them.

I view the gallery space as a three-dimensional essay, with exhibition design acting as curatorial writing. As a curator, I strive to create an exchange between artwork, space, and the viewer. I’m fascinated by how spaces can be activated, structured and transformed into environments that sustain dialogue with those who experience them.

This spatial approach is most evident during exhibition installation. When positioning works, I construct the viewer’s experience, considering their initial encounter, movement through the room and what lingers with them. Intentional distance and negative space are used to allow heavy themes like grief and loss to breathe. I also recognise that a photograph transforms depending on its surroundings, whether isolated, next to a tactile textile or across from an immersive audio installation.

Collaboration and interpretation

I'm particularly interested in collaborative and interdisciplinary practices that engage with archives, photography, spatial interventions, and socially embedded forms of research. My goal is to curate exhibitions that actively engage visitors in the relationships between artwork, space and each other rather than passive spectators.

It’s equally crucial to allow diverse emotional responses to coexist within the exhibition so that each piece can fully express its meaning and emotional impact, not merely as an isolated object on display.

Whether working with historical collections, personal archives, or newly commissioned works, I approach curating as the construction of relationships between artworks, people, and space. Rather than producing fixed interpretations, I seek to create exhibitions that activate overlooked narratives through spatial relationships. This approach creates space for negotiation, allowing diverse histories and lived experiences to coexist without collapsing into a single narrative.
