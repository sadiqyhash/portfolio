Curatorial practice

Working with space, research and memory.

My curatorial approach is rooted in spatial thinking, documentary methods and alternative forms of archiving. I understand exhibition-making as a process of research and translation, where the space itself becomes part of the work and exhibition design functions as a form of curatorial writing.

Method

Each project begins with a specific question rather than a broad theme. I develop the question through conversations, site-specific investigation, sustained research, interviews, archival and fieldwork, and exchange with artists, participants and communities.

My background in architectural engineering informs how I consider movement, relationships, context and the physical conditions of an exhibition. I approach the exhibition space as a living structure: a place where artworks, documents, voices and audiences encounter one another, and where the arrangement of material shapes how a story can be read.

I am interested in curatorial processes that make room for complexity, multiple perspectives and overlooked histories, while remaining attentive to the responsibilities involved in representing people and their experiences.
