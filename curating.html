Curatorial practice

Working with space, research and memory.

My curatorial practice brings together spatial thinking, documentary research, and alternative forms of archiving. With a background in architectural engineering, I approach exhibitions as spaces where artworks, histories, and people enter into dialogue.

I begin with questions rather than themes, working through conversations, archives, field research, and close engagement with artists and communities. I’m interested in how exhibition-making can bring overlooked narratives into view without reducing complex experiences to a single story.

For me, space is not simply where an exhibition happens. It is part of how the story is told.

Selected curatorial projects

Photography exhibition · 2024

Roots of Identity

Jordan National Gallery of Fine Arts · Amman, Jordan Goethe-Institut – EU Curator

Roots of Identity: Yemeni Photo Exhibition brings together photographic perspectives on Yemen’s cultural heritage and the ways identity is represented amid conflict and disruption. The exhibition creates space for Yemeni artists to tell their own stories beyond reductive portrayals of the country.

Mixed-media exhibition · 2025

SAWA

Jordan National Gallery of Fine Arts · Amman, Jordan Goethe-Institut – CARPO Curator

SAWA (“together” in Arabic) brings together work by thirteen emerging Yemeni artists and practitioners across photography, film, comics, illustration, podcasts, music, and community practice. The exhibition explores how creative work builds connections and makes room for shared experiences and futures.

Mixed-media residency · 2025

Mycelium

IDEA Festival · Amman, Jordan Guest curator

Mycelium is a cross-border artistic residency presented as part of IDEA Festival 2025 and programmed by Studio 8 in collaboration with Danseurs Citoyens Sud (DCS) and INTERFERENCE. Building on a regional journey that began in 2021, the residency brings emerging Arab artists together through interdisciplinary exchange, experimentation, and co-creation.

Inspired by the underground networks that connect and sustain ecosystems, Mycelium imagines artistic collaboration as a living, decentralised network—one that nurtures dialogue, shared resources, and connections across the MENA region.
