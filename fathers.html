What Do Fathers Leave Behind?

2023 — Ongoing

Documentary photography, family archive, sound, and text

Text

I’ve spent my life searching for my father, trying to piece together the fragments he left behind. I’ve longed to hear his stories, his adventures, sacrifices, and countless journeys away from our village. He was the one person I could never fully reach, the one who never told me all I needed to hear, the one who left too soon. Yet, in my search for my father, my mother became the keeper of all the stories, experiences, and tales about him, and about herself. She told me about my father’s village, a place I was meant to call home but one she despised, sacrificing everything for him and for us. As I sought my own identity and sense of belonging, I discovered that my mother had become a stranger to her own identity. Through my father, and later through us, his children, she became immersed in a different society and culture. She lost touch with her history and clung to scattered remnants of her former self. My mother shared countless stories about my father’s struggles and his life away from her. I often wondered how she knew so much. How my father could have left her with all these tales, as though entrusting her with the responsibility of passing them on to us.

Through my quest to find my father, I unknowingly wove connections between the three of us, my father, my mother, and myself, through memory and place. Along this journey, I became a blend of both my parents, carrying my father’s features and my mother’s emotions and stories.

Whenever I yearned for my father, I turned to my memories and to our family album a collection of no more than fifty photos, most of which didn’t include him. After losing my father, I became increasingly drawn to exploring my mother’s life, documenting her stories and songs. His absence compelled me to delve into the remaining physical archives of our family. But I wonder: does the past exist as we remember it? How far does memory stretch across the distance created by time? Grief is universal, yet my experience of losing my father at a young age and grappling with fragmented memories feels deeply personal. It has led me to engage in a dialogue about who owns memory, who owns identity, and who bears the burden of creating shared experiences of fragmentation simply to survive in the world. I often reflect on how we perceive, explore, and ultimately claim our identities. My ongoing project, What Do Fathers Leave Behind? Tracing Absence, is a materialization of loss—the loss of a father, the loss of identity, and the loss of my father’s and my own past and future selves. How does one create an archive after years of absence? How does one take a self-portrait without a self? Through this project, I explore the relationship between identity, loss, belonging, and memory, using family archives, photography, writing, and sound. I am interested in the intersections of these media and how they can work together as tools to create an experimental memory landscape.
